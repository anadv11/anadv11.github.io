## About the presentation

Degrowth policies seek to integrate social and ecological issues, bringing social justice and ecological sustainability together. In this sense, they can be considered as a paramount case of eco-social policies. Building on the current cross-pollination between the overlapping bodies of research on degrowth and eco-social policy, this paper proposes a conceptualization of degrowth policy objectives and then places them in the context of a degrowth transition through a relational approach that distinguishes between two generations of eco-social policies.

First, the paper proposes a conceptualization of degrowth policies identifying their core dimensions based on a literature review of existing taxonomies and of degrowth literature in the context of the MAPS project. The objectives of degrowth policies are complemented with a relational approach that distinguishes between two generations of eco-social policy based on sequencing and political potential: 1st Generation eco-social policies, focused on universal need satisfaction through the decommodification of provisioning systems, and 2nd Generation eco-social policies, focused on collective self-limitation within biophysical limits. Given that decommodification of need satisfaction is a necessary but not sufficient condition for enacting degrowth, procedural eco-social policies are emphasized beyond the substantial, paying attention to the democratic institutional architecture and the cultural change that allows the shift from 1st Generation to 2nd Generation eco-social policies. 

Based on this theoretical framework, a multicriteria toolbox is presented to explore context-specific degrowth policy packages sensitive to structural dimensions (welfare regime) and conjunctural analysis (political potential for broad alliances). The framework is applied to the case of Spain, sketching a 1st Generation eco-social policy package for enabling a degrowth transition.
