With Filka Sekulova and Federico Demaria (Universitat de Barcelona, School of Economics). Presented in the session *OSS 6.7.A: Exploring Degrowth Societies: Future Scenarios and Policy Mixes* at the 16th Conference of the European Society for Ecological Economics (ESEE2026), Ghent, 1 July 2026 (15:30–17:00).

The conference theme was *Looking for (the) Just Judges – Promoting Sustainable and Inclusive Well-Being in Turbulent Times* (30 June – 3 July 2026).

## Abstract

This article advances the emerging field of degrowth public policy by applying a policy design lens to existing degrowth policy proposals. Building on recent inventories and taxonomies of degrowth and eco-social policies, it extracts core features used in categorising degrowth policies: democratic governance, sustainable scale, good life for all, intersectional equity, pluriversal justice, and convivial imaginaries. The article then evaluates the degrowth policies identified in the literature, assessing both their alignment with degrowth policy principles and their expected social and ecological impacts. A further step examines complementarities between policies, responding to calls for the exploration of policy interactions. The article ultimately proposes a coherent potential policy mix for Europe that reflects degrowth principles while addressing the full social-ecological spectrum. The results aim to support future advocacy, modelling, and empirical evaluation of post-growth strategies.

## About the paper

The policy projects of the degrowth movement are currently under construction, with scholars calling for increasing attention to the role of the state in degrowth strategies. In the process of building up a degrowth approach to the state and policymaking, the public policy design literature can be of help. Michael Howlett (2023) suggests applying social science model building to the policy design process, outlining several steps: (1) developing inventories of policies, (2) constructing taxonomies, (3) building theoretical models, (4) evaluating policies and (5) developing prescriptive studies. In recent years, the degrowth community has tackled steps one and two, developing comprehensive inventories of degrowth and eco-social policies, as well as taxonomies to classify such policies into themes and objectives.

This article is part of Horizon Europe project Models, Assessment, and Policies for Sustainability (MAPS). It builds upon these reviews while taking the degrowth policy literature a step further, by engaging with steps three and four in Howlett’s framework. After synthesising existing taxonomies, the article develops a framework for theoretical evaluation (step 3) that defines what a degrowth policy is, or could be, and then evaluates the policies (step 4) based on their fit with the framework and their complementarity with each other. The results are then used to create a degrowth policy mix for Europe that comprehensively covers social and environmental aspects while considering policy interactions.

Because most degrowth policies in the literature have not yet been implemented, empirical evaluation is not possible and conceptual evaluation becomes essential. The article therefore:

1. Develops a framework for evaluation based on a review of degrowth, political ecology and ecological economics literature, identifying key features of degrowth (democratic governance, sustainable scale, good life for all, intersectional equity, pluriversal justice, and convivial imaginaries).
2. Uses this framework to evaluate policies derived from previous inventories and taxonomies, examining their social and ecological impacts.
3. Assesses potential interactions and complementarities between the policies, responding to calls for further exploration of policy interaction.

Finally, using the evaluation of policies and their interactions, the article proposes a coherent and hypothetical policy mix for degrowth. This mix fits with the ontological tenets of what a degrowth policy is. The outcomes can serve as a starting point for further advocacy work and can inform further modelling and evaluation.
